# What the Export / World Edition (Western) Community Knew (2002-2011)

KEY: `community-english-we`
Scope: the export/Western DOC scene's own understanding — the World Edition game structure, the
handbooks' read of foods/training/breeding, the symbol legend, the leg-type rule, the jackpot /
Best Right method, and the exact in-game English wording they recorded — each claim tagged. Read
`community-overview` first for the tag system and hedging rule.

The English-speaking community got the game two to three years late, imported half its knowledge
from Hong Kong and Japan (see `community-hongkong`), organised the rest around the American export
game, and wrote its own handbooks. These sources describe **World Edition (Rev A-C) and its 2005
update World Edition EX (Rev D)** — the export game most Western cabinets ran, including most of
ours. It is a *different game* from the Japanese wing: different race structure, renamed venues
and races, a printed G1 earnings gate, and a scene, an economy and an ending that happened nowhere
else. **Numbers do not transfer casually between the two wings.** Recovered on the vetted English
wing at doc.johnreevesiii.com/preservation/english; the findings belong to the players, handbook
authors and stable-site owners named there by their public handles (doc.rbcb.net / "Oop's Corner"
by OOP, the michaelpule.com Handbooks by Michael Pule "the Horse", RUN Stables, Shety Stables,
IS Guise, ATXStables, and others).

## One structural caution about this corpus

**Most of the English corpus is one textual lineage.** The Shety Stables pages, the World of
HEEHEE blog, the handbooks, RUN Stables, and the IS Guise roster share sentences, tables, and even
typos (the shared misspelling "formitable"). The Handbook says so plainly: "I have taken things
from the forum... nothing in here is from my own mouth." **When five of these sources agree, that
is one source, not five.** And the corpus is partly translated Asian knowledge — OOP credits the
internals model to "the Hong Kong Boys," the Great Escape charts to expertdoc, and the handbook's
"Tokyo whipping charts" survive as Office files whose metadata names a Japanese author. So
English/Japanese agreement is often the same knowledge twice, not independent corroboration.

## The export game's structure

- **Sixteen rounds, six races each, G1 last — COMMUNITY CLAIM (corroborated).** The World Edition
  season is a 16-round rotation. Each round is six races: 1R (a Handicap), 2R, 3R ("Special,"
  higher purse), 4R, 5R, and the **round's G1 as race 6**. Multiply attested in the main lineage,
  independently by FunTrivia, and consistent with Sega's own printed schedule (six races per
  program, race 6 the feature). The Japanese versions run the G1 as 8R; no recovered English
  source even mentions the Japanese 8R structure.
- **The six venues — COMMUNITY CLAIM, DISPUTED on the count.** Eastern City (E.C.), Western Hill
  (W.H.), Northern Park (N.P.), Central City (C.C.), Southern Park (S.P.), and a sixth venue
  literally named **Sega (SEGA)**, which hosts the Super Dirt Grand Prix and the season-closing
  Derby Owners Cup. The official US race schedule names five; the community consistently used six.
  Whether "Sega" is a distinct venue-table entry or a special label on two races is an open ROM
  check. Localisation lore (community claim, via FunTrivia): the Japan Cups run at Eastern City
  because Eastern City is Tokyo, the "Eastern Capital," renamed.
- **The $1,000,000 G1 gate — OFFICIAL (Sega's own print).** The Sega World Edition sales brochure
  (NTRA co-branded, c. 2002), page 7, verbatim: "A simulated day at the track consists of 6 races.
  Races 1-5 are open to all comers... Race 6 is reserved for G-1 eligible horses. To qualify for a
  G-1 race, your horse must have lifetime earnings of $1 million dollars or more." The community
  matched it exactly (the 2004 Handbooks; FunTrivia). Its on-screen face is the string "Grade 1
  Eligible." (OFFICIAL and community-corroborated; the ROM check itself has not been located, so
  it is not additionally CONFIRMED.) A related COMMUNITY CLAIM: every round's 1R is a Handicap race
  whose weight is set by total earnings only, "not related to your wins or the number of races."

- **The sixteen G1s, as the community recorded them — COMMUNITY CLAIM** (one lineage: RUN Stables,
  the Mini Handbook, Shety's grid, Oop's Corner; checkable against the WE ROM schedule table):

- **Hong Kong 6R region mode — CONFIRMED.** The "Hong Kong/American" double-naming period sources
  recorded is not a dispute or a dual label — it is a **region setting**. A World Edition machine
  put into Hong Kong 6R mode substitutes the **Hong Kong Derby (race option 17)** for the American
  Derby (round 7) and the **Hong Kong Oaks (race option 18)** for the American Oaks (round 6): same
  slots, same distances and rules, different name, not new races. That is exactly why the period
  sources saw both names for the same two events.
- **Retirement/career arc — OFFICIAL + COMMUNITY CLAIM.** The WE brochure (OFFICIAL): after the
  20th race you are asked whether to retire; a horse "will no longer be competitive (this usually
  occurs between 30-45 races)." The community (COMMUNITY CLAIM): retire at 20 if breeding is the
  goal; mares slow around 28 races, males around 30. (Roughly consistent with the project's
  race-driven growth-plateau finding of ~25-30 races, but framed differently — a growth plateau is
  not the same claim as "slowing down"; do not merge them.)

## What they knew about the machine

- **The leg-type rule — CONFIRMED (first flagship agreement).** The English community had it
  exactly right from at least 2003 (OOP, doc.rbcb.net): "The type of horse you run (Front Runner,
  Start Dash, Last Spurt, Stretch Runner) is determined by where your Start is. When figuring this
  out, do not count your Cornering stat. If Start is the highest you will be a Front Runner; second
  highest, Start Dash; third highest, Last Spurt; fourth highest or worse, Stretch Runner." That is
  precisely the rule the project's SH-4 decode recovered — running style is Start's rank among the
  five non-Corner externals — plus the fifth case, Almighty when all are equal, and the tie-break
  (Start wins ties). There is even an OFFICIAL capstone (Sega's English FAQ, 2002): "Front-runner,
  Start dash, Last spurt, Stretch-runner and Almighty. The horse leg type will be set as Almighty
  when the grade of five abilities except Corner are same points." **Community, developer, and ROM
  all agree — the strongest-attested mechanic in either wing.** Cultural twist: where the Japanese
  scene chased Almighty, the English scene told players to *avoid* it (HEEHEE, 2004: "There is NO
  effective whipping technique for these horses, so avoid getting them always!!") — same rule,
  opposite doctrine, because the English game was a whip-chart culture and a style with no chart
  had no value.
- **The floor-average breeding formula — CONFIRMED (second flagship).** OOP's Corner, "Retirement
  Stats": "Retirement stats are Sire + Dam / 2 (rounded down)... Fixed at birth. No amount of
  training, foods, or winning will raise an external stat." Canonical worked example: Thunder Boy
  (11 11 4 6 13 11) × Butter Popcorn (9 7 7 11 9 13) always gives 10 9 5 8 11 12. Same operation as
  the Japanese June-2000 finding and the project's SH-4 decode. But do not count the English
  statement as a third *independent* derivation — the English corpus is downstream of the Hong Kong
  material. The English record's own myth-kill (Haynekine) against the belief that racing raises
  breeding stats: "You will never turn a single circle horse into a double circle."
- **The symbol legend — COMMUNITY CLAIM (two consistent lines).** The numeric sire/dam tables
  print symbol and number side by side, resolving the mapping: **✕ = 1-4, △ (triangle) = 5-8,
  ○ (circle) = 9-12, ◎ (double circle) = 13-16.** This is the same hidden 1-16 scale the Japanese
  16-Step Theory described, with the same off-by-one caution — treat 1-16 as the community's
  display-side model of a 0-63 byte drawn in 16 blocks (see `community-japanese`).
- **Best Right — the jackpot method (COMMUNITY CLAIM, US origin).** The cleverest piece of English
  play-science, and a US addition absent from every Hong Kong source. Best Right is a CPU benchmark
  horse that reliably appears in specific 4R races and "pulls all double circles when no players
  are present." Unable to see internals, the community built a measurement instrument out of him:
  take a fresh foal from a maxed line, give it exactly two solo trainings with "Cool" results and
  plain foods, and enter its second race in a 4R where Best Right appears — if your debutant steals
  Best Right's first- or third-column double circles (Stamina or Sharp) it is a "Jackpot" horse;
  one stolen circle is a "Mini-Jackpot"; the second (Speed) circle "is too strong to steal." This
  is the English equivalent of the Japanese "the odds leak the hidden stats" trick: both learned to
  read the game's own evaluation of hidden state off a public display. Best Right's actual numbers
  are **DISPUTED** — folklore put his internals around 52/60/54; a 2022 ROM reading gives 31/53/51.
- **The roster warning — DISPUTED.** The community's numeric sire/dam rosters **do not match the
  ROM**. A 2022 ROM reading gives Maple Syrup's externals as 15/3/6/10/4/12 where the Shety/IS
  Guise table gives 15/4/8/9/5/13 — same shape, different numbers; the 2004 rosters were almost
  certainly observational (read off gauges and breeding results). Treat community rosters only as
  the community's working model, never as ground truth.

## What the English community never found

The gaps matter as much as the hits. Nothing recovered in the entire English corpus mentions: the
~1-in-32 jackpot/dud bands on foal internals; the 45 internal birth cap; the internals-sum-160
growth wall on Rev D; the hearts thresholds gating internal growth (or hearts as anything more than
a relationship meter); the training damper driven by the externals total; or that running style is
recomputed before every race rather than stored (they knew the rule but not that it is re-run).
Where the Japanese scene was a mechanics-research culture, the English scene was a technique-and-
logistics culture: whip charts, schedules, records, tournaments, trading.

## Exact English wording, recovered from the game (COMMUNITY CLAIM unless tagged)

A deliberate assignment of this wing: recover the export game's on-screen English, [sic] and all,
because the export localisation's errors are themselves findings.

- **Birth comments.** The best birth comment is "Your horse has a bright feature" — the in-game
  misspelling of "bright future" (attested first-hand on doc.rbcb.net, where a player who typed
  "future" was corrected twice). The game also misspells "Dam" as "Dame" and "Jockey" as "Jocky."
  A partial ladder roughly worst to best (order disputed in-thread): "meet your expectation,"
  "your horse really meet your expectation," "your horse has real thoroughbred body," "potential is
  excellent," "Your horse is a gifted horse," "bright feature"; worst of all is no comment. (Same
  design as the Japanese nine-tier ladder, rougher localisation.)
- **The five characters and their farm tells (COMMUNITY CLAIM).** Identified by the corral
  animation: Imposing (rears up), Rough (mule kick), Honest (shakes head and body), Coward
  (startled, shimmies), Sloppy (lays down, falls asleep). "Shy" is a FunTrivia decoy, not a sixth
  character. Birth announcements: Imposing = "Your Horse has a Reagal Presence" [sic], Rough =
  "Your Horse is a naughty Horse."
- **The care menu.** As the menus printed it: Blandish, Praise, Encourage, Flatter, Apologize,
  Comfort, Hug, Ignore, Leave, Soothe, Psyche Up, Astonish, Scold. Two full response guides survive
  (Matt Barlow's "Keep Your Hearts" on RUN Stables; Ayelis's Relationship Guide v0.6 on GameFAQs).
- **Post-race strings, Mini Handbook 2004 (spelling as printed):** "Looks Happy after winning the
  Race" · "Very Happy to captured a Great victory" [sic] · "Dissapointed After Losing Race" [sic] ·
  "Angry because you used the whip too Much" · "Won the G1 even with strong rivals" · "Doesn't
  Trust you."
- **Status / UI strings.** "Grade 1 Eligible" (the $1M gate's face); "Do you bet the Farm?" (the
  retirement wind-down question, ~race 35); training's top grade "Great!!" (ladder Great / Cool /
  Good); the race HUD "HORSE CONDITION" and "WHIP EFFECT (LOW)" anchor the whip vocabulary — a
  "Rocket Start" is whipping from the gate to the "H" in HORSE CONDITION, a "Super Start" to the
  "C," and the "W hit" a whip timed to the bar falling back to the "w" in "low." Co-op training's
  target is a "Dead Heat."

## The WE-vs-EX fork and the glitch-horse era

- **The 2005 EX changelog — OFFICIAL.** The segaamusements.com "Derby Owners Club Ex" product page:
  a new race call by announcer Richard Grunder, 30 new sires and 30 new dams, tournament software
  with 3 competition modes, on-screen display of your 6 prior race results, and a new "classic
  Trumpeter Call to Post" attract audio.
- **The fork as players felt it — COMMUNITY CLAIM.** EX renamed sires and dams "to make it more
  difficult to identify the strong breeding pairs" (e.g. Butter Popcorn → "Layin Back," Ferranti's
  Folly → "Sarabeara," Lucky Laurie → "Peach Girl"). EX also handicapped over-developed young
  horses, and the jackpot definition changed (WE = steal a symbol from Best Right; EX = any symbols
  drawn in the first race). Version tell taught to players: "If you see EX on the screen [at the
  pre-race logo], you are running EX software."
- **Double-circle "glitch aristocracy" — COMMUNITY CLAIM, DISPUTED mechanism.** All-double-circle
  horses "came into existence in Hong Kong": DOC 2000 v.2 horses with all-✕ stats run on World
  Edition machines wrapped past the bottom into all double circles, "based on binary math" (OOP),
  then were duplicated and sold on eBay. Claimed properties: DC horses mature much faster, stop
  responding to training by 5-10 races, are indistinguishable from a normal horse by ~20, and can't
  be bred onward usefully (OOP's blunt verdict on breeding one down: "It's a waste of time. And
  money. Live with it."). The freewebs Nashville page closes the arc: EX was "the patch released by
  Sega to stop the Glitch horses that were sold on Ebay." The underflow mechanism is testable
  against the card byte format — treat it as claimed, not confirmed.
- **The revision record underneath — CONFIRMED.** At the ROM-image level: World Edition Revs A, B
  and C differ only in the boot EPROM (all mask ROMs identical); Rev D (EX) changes the boot EPROM
  plus two mask ROMs — which lines up with the official changelog.

## The scene: whip charts, tournaments, the card economy, the end (COMMUNITY CLAIM unless tagged)

- **Whip charts** were the English scene's native art form: per-distance, per-leg-type schedules of
  exactly when to whip and hold. "The Great Escape" (front-runner meta chart, credited "Thanks to
  expertdoc in Hong Kong"); "Wing's Menu" (the most-referenced chart, deliberately never posted).
  Read against the project's race decode before believing the mechanics: the confirmed whip
  findings (one grade-blind whip input, a 16-tick refractory lockout, placement worth nothing at
  matched whip count) mean the charts can't work for the reason the community thought — but spaced
  whips at counted intervals are exactly what a refractory lockout rewards, so the technique may
  have been right while the theory was wrong. OOP's line every arcade rat needed: "If you are
  losing close races, it is not your horse that is the problem... it is your jockey skills."
- **Tournaments on two continents.** OFFICIAL: the Sega May Derby (May 2004), a UK national
  tournament Sega ran itself across nine sites with a £4,500 purse and text-message registration —
  the only known case of Sega running a national DOC tournament in the West. COMMUNITY CLAIM: the
  2004 DOC National Championship (Chicago, April 24-25, 2004, $110 entry), 2005 Nationals
  (Philadelphia), and various regional cups. The "tournament horse" was its own doctrine: because
  G1 (tournament) mode never writes back to the card, players built "convertible" horses tuned for
  the 16-G1 loop but undamaged by it. Crowded 8-seat venues produced written etiquette codes
  ("Thou shall not race any horse over 15 races in 2R, 4R and 5R").
- **The card-editing arc.** OFFICIAL: Sega's June 18, 2004 Digital Bulletin 1 warned operators that
  players had bought master keys on eBay, opened the card-reader and service doors, and cheated by
  swapping their card out after losses (so only wins were written back) and granting themselves
  service credits; Sega's fix was to replace the locks. Card-editing tools followed on the community
  side (the "Super Juicer" editing workbook, metadata dated 2005; a commercial "DOC Card Modifier"
  VB6 app shipped 2007), and a paid editing service ("Horse Juicing," $5 a horse) ran on Shety
  Stables — the editing scene and the knowledge scene were often the same
  people. The period tell for a manufactured card, on both continents, was the same: a record and
  earnings profile that does not add up.
- **The business — OFFICIAL.** The WE brochure: three US test games averaged over $850 per day;
  average price per play $1.75; "93% of DOC players are starting their game with an existing DOC
  card." The NTRA licensed DOC WE as its official interactive video game in 2002; official Sega
  service for the NAOMI DOC machines ended March 31, 2017.
- **The end of the venues.** Removals begin 2004-2005; Dave & Buster's employees cite lack of play,
  floor space, and above all cards ("due to cards no longer made... no card, it just hangs"). The
  last public US machine left a Dave & Buster's in 2021; in June 2025 one returned to a public floor
  in Gainesville FL. First-person color worth keeping: players "carried FULL PHOTO ALBUMS full of
  cards," "three-ring binders full of horse cards, kinda like MtG players." The cards outlived the
  venues, and the knowledge outlived the cards.

## The living-source advantage (why this wing can still grow)

Unlike the Japanese wing, several English-scene people are reachable today and credited by public
role: Michael Pule ("the Horse") hosts a cabinet in our own arcade and can be interviewed about
doc.rbcb.net, the handbook sources, and the lost horse database; Ted (ATXStables / Virtual Arcades)
is active and archiving; and the modern emulation/decode authors are all reachable. Oral history is
the one recovery method the Wayback Machine cannot do.
